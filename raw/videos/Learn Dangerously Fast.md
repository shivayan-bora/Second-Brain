## Problems with the most common learning advice

- **Myth**: If you don't understand something, go over it again?
  - **Actual Problem**: Your process is limiting you to understand the topic.
  - **Why?** You don't have enough context around that topic and your mind doesn't know what to connect it with.
  - **Solution**:
    - Take sometime to build the context.
    - How? Zoom out and ask questions to see the big picture.
      - Why is this is this important?
      - How can I use and apply this information?
      - What is the main point of this in its simplest form?
      - What would I need to already know in order for this to become easier?
- **Myth**: Learn at your own pace.
  - **Actual Problem**: Encourages learning to be comfortable and not move to the next step until you feel ready.
    - Atrophies growth because you become risk and mistake averse.
  - **Why?** The best learning happens when we get feedback which happens when we make mistakes.
  - **Solution**:
    - Embrace making mistakes and learn at the pace where you're making meaningful mistakes
    - Make learning balanced where you're learning concepts slightly above your current level.
      - Make the learning too easy and you're bored, make it too difficult and you're overwhelmed.
- **Myth**: Summarize what you've learned.
  - **Actual**: Summarization doesn't follow good encoding and retrieval practices which are the corner stone of a good learning technique.
  - **Solution**:
    - Summarize the right way with Active Recall.
    - Anything which forces you to process information while forming meaningful connections, groups and relationships while getting connected to the big picture.
    - Actively testing yourself with active recall which is not cue-based and from memory.
- **Myth**: If something is overwhelming break it down into smaller pieces.
  - **Actual Problem**: Doesn't allow us to see the big picture and form meaningful connections when we look at a group of information in isolation.
  - **Solution**:
    - Priming: Firstly look at the contents, headings, sub-headings etc. to figure out which are the concepts available in the topic.
    - Start forming initial connections and then layer the other pieces of information over it step by step to form the entire big picture and make meaningful connections.
- **Myth**: If learning something is hard, make it easier.
  - **Actual Problem**: This is risk averseness. Learning is supposed to feel hard since it allows us to form schema through desirable difficulty.
  - **Solution**:
    - If learning something is hard, learn how to learn hard things.

## Key Takeaways

- A good knowledge is when you have a strong interconnected network of knowledge.
  - It helps you see patterns and allows you make efficient at solving problems and when encountered with a new piece of information, learn that quickly since you're able to connect that new piece of information to your existing schema of knowledge.
- This is why we have learning techniques.
- For a learning technique to be effective, it needs to have two components:
  - Encoding: Ability to connect a new piece of information to our existing schema of knowledge
  - Recall: Where we're testing ourselves with what we've learned which is free and uncued.
    - Ideally whatever we're learning, we should be able to apply that and solve problems even when we haven't encountered a problem before.
- Learning by nature is hard and we won't learn unless we apply our knowledge to face challenges.
  - If a challenge is too easy, we would get bored and if it's too difficult then, we would be overwhelmed.
  - There should be a decent ratio of correct hits to mistakes and this balance is known as desirable difficulty.
- Key pointers:
  - Prime yourself with whatever you want to learn understanding what the key concepts in that topic are.
    - One tip is to look at the content, headings subheadings to get a list of keywords in that concept.
    - Try to form an initial schema with whatever information you have currently to try to fit the new concepts in your schema.
  - Go through the material and test yourself to validate your current schema and course correct accordingly.
  - Repeat this cycle and layer your knowledge concept by concept.
  - Embrace the fact that learning is hard and you will make mistakes both of which are necessary for an effective learning session.
